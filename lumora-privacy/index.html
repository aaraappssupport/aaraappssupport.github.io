Privacy Policy for Lumora

Last updated: 28 August 2026

Lumora is a reminder and personal organiser app for Android, published by the developer of Lumora (“we”, “us”). This policy explains what Lumora does with your information. It is short because Lumora does very little with it.

Lumora does not collect, transmit, or store your personal data on any server.

Every reminder, note, time, category and completion record you create stays in a database on your own device. We cannot see it. We have no servers that hold it, no account system that identifies you, and no analytics, advertising or crash reporting service that observes you.

There is one exception, and it is optional: the weather card described below.

What stays on your device

All of the following is stored only in Lumora’s private storage on your phone, and is never sent anywhere:

  • Reminders — titles, notes, dates, times, repeat rules, categories, priorities
  • Any details you enter into a template’s fields, such as a medicine name and dose, or a passport number
  • Your completion history and streaks
  • Your display name, if you choose to set one
  • Your theme, appearance and notification preferences

Android’s own app-sandbox protections apply to this data: no other app can read it.

The optional weather card

Lumora can show current weather on the home screen. It is off until you choose a city, and you can turn it off again at any time in More → Weather.

When it is on:

  • Lumora sends the coordinates of the city you selected from a list to Open-Meteo, and receives the current conditions.
  • While searching for your city, Lumora sends the text you type to Open-Meteo’s geocoding service so it can offer matching places.
  • Nothing else is sent. No reminder, note, title, time, device identifier, advertising ID or account travels with the request. There is no identifier of any kind attached, so Open-Meteo has no way to connect two requests as coming from the same person.
  • The connection uses HTTPS.

Open-Meteo is an independent service with its own terms; see open-meteo.com/en/terms.

Lumora never asks for your device location. It holds no location permission of any kind. You tell it a city by name; the phone is never asked where it is.

What Lumora does not do

  • No user accounts, sign-in, or profiles
  • No analytics or usage tracking of any kind
  • No crash or error reporting to us or to any third party
  • No advertising, and no advertising identifiers
  • No third-party SDK that collects data
  • No selling or sharing of data with anyone — there is nothing to sell
  • No access to your contacts, photos, files, microphone, camera, calendar, call logs or messages

Children

Lumora is not directed at children under 13 and collects no data from anyone, including children.

Your data rights

Because your data never leaves your device, there is nothing for us to retrieve, correct, export or delete on your behalf — we have no copy.

  • To delete everything: uninstall Lumora, or use Android’s Settings → Apps → Lumora → Storage → Clear storage. This is immediate, permanent, and complete.
  • Note: Lumora currently offers no backup, export or cloud sync. Deleting the app deletes your reminders permanently and they cannot be recovered.

Data retention

Your data is retained on your device for exactly as long as you keep the app installed, and is destroyed when you uninstall it or clear its storage. We hold nothing, so we retain nothing.

Security

Lumora contains no API keys, tokens, passwords or other secrets. Data is held in Android’s private per-app storage, isolated from other apps by the operating system. All network traffic (the weather card alone) uses HTTPS.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the “Last updated” date above changes with it. If a future version of Lumora ever collects data — for example, if optional cloud sync is added — this policy will be updated before that version is released, and the change will be described in the app’s release notes.
