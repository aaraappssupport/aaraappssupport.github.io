Privacy Policy for Mementa

Effective date: 20 October 2026
Mementa is published by AARA Apps. It was previously named Lumora.

Mementa is a reminder and personal organiser app for Android. This policy explains what Mementa does with your information. It is short because Mementa does very little with it.

We never receive your personal data. Mementa has no servers to receive it.

Every reminder, note, time, category and completion record you create is kept in a database on your own device. We cannot see it. We have no servers that hold it, no account system that identifies you, and no analytics, advertising or crash-reporting service that observes you.

Two things can send data off the phone, and you control both: the optional weather card, and Android’s own backup of your phone. Both are described below.

What stays on your device

All of the following is stored in Mementa’s private storage on your phone. Mementa itself never sends it anywhere. The only copy that can leave the phone is Android’s own backup, described in the next section.

  • Reminders: titles, notes, dates, times, repeat rules, categories and priorities
  • Anything you enter into a template’s fields, such as a medicine name and dose, or a passport number
  • Your completion history and streaks
  • Your display name, if you choose to set one
  • Your theme, appearance, weather and notification preferences
  • The last weather reading and the city you chose, if you use the weather card

Android’s app sandbox protects this data: other apps cannot read it.

If you add Mementa’s home-screen widget, it shows your upcoming reminders on your home screen, so anyone who can see your home screen can see them. You choose whether to add it.

Android backup

If backup is turned on for your phone, Android includes Mementa’s data in it, as it does for other apps, so your reminders come back when you set up a new phone. This is Android’s feature, run by Google under your Google Account. Mementa does not send the data, and we have no way to access it.

  • On Android 9 and later, Mementa’s data goes into the cloud backup only when that backup can be end-to-end encrypted with your phone’s screen lock (PIN, pattern or password). Neither Google nor we can read it. With no screen lock set, Mementa’s data is left out of the cloud backup.
  • On Android 7 and 8, Android encrypts the backup but does not end-to-end encrypt it.
  • Copying your data directly from an old phone to a new one during setup is a local transfer between the two devices and follows Android’s default behaviour.
  • You can turn backup off at any time in your phone’s backup settings. Google keeps only the most recent backup.

The optional weather card

Mementa can show the current weather at the top of the Today screen. It is off until you choose a city, and you can turn it off at any time with the Show weather switch in More → Weather.

When it is on:

  • Mementa sends the coordinates of the city you selected from a list to Open-Meteo (api.open-meteo.com), and receives the current temperature and conditions. The coordinates are rounded to four decimal places.
  • While you search for your city, Mementa sends the text you type to Open-Meteo’s geocoding service (geocoding-api.open-meteo.com) so it can offer matching places.
  • Mementa refreshes the reading at most every 30 minutes, and only while the Today screen is open and the app is in the foreground. If the service cannot be reached, the last reading stored on your phone is shown instead.
  • Nothing else is sent. No reminder, note, title, time, device identifier, advertising ID or account travels with the request, and Mementa attaches no identifier of its own. As with any request over the internet, Open-Meteo’s servers can see your phone’s IP address, and handle it under their own policy.
  • The connection uses HTTPS.

When Show weather is off, or when no city has been chosen, Mementa makes no weather request at all. The one exception is the city search: if you open the city picker and type in its search box, the text you type is sent to Open-Meteo’s geocoding service whether or not the switch is on.

Open-Meteo is an independent service with its own terms; see open-meteo.com/en/terms.

Mementa never asks for your device location. It holds no location permission of any kind. You tell it a city by name; the phone is never asked where it is.

What Mementa does not do

  • No user accounts, sign-in or profiles
  • No analytics or usage tracking of any kind
  • No crash or error reporting to us or to any third party
  • No advertising, and no advertising identifiers
  • No third-party SDK that collects data
  • No selling or sharing of data with anyone; there is nothing to sell
  • No access to your contacts, photos, files, microphone, camera, calendar, call logs or messages

Your phone may also list keep the device awake, view network connections and run foreground service. Mementa does not request these itself: they come from Android’s own WorkManager library, which keeps the home-screen widget up to date. Mementa starts no foreground service and uses none of these three to collect anything.

Children

Mementa is not directed at children under 13 and collects no data from anyone, including children.

Your data rights

We never receive your data, so there is nothing for us to retrieve, correct, export or delete on your behalf. We have no copy.

  • To delete everything on the phone: uninstall Mementa, or use Android’s Settings → Apps → Mementa → Storage → Clear storage.
  • If Android backup is on, a copy also exists in your Google Account’s backup. Delete it, or turn backup off, from your phone’s backup settings or your Google Account.
  • Note: Mementa itself offers no export or cloud sync. Without Android backup, uninstalling deletes your reminders permanently and they cannot be recovered.

Data retention

Your data is kept on your device for as long as you keep the app installed, and is destroyed when you uninstall it or clear its storage. If Android backup is on, Google keeps the most recent backup in your Google Account under its own retention rules. We hold nothing, so we retain nothing.

Security

Mementa contains no API keys, tokens, passwords or other secrets. Data is held in Android’s private per-app storage, isolated from other apps by the operating system. All network traffic (the weather card alone) uses HTTPS.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the effective date above changes with it. If a future version of Mementa ever collects data, for example if optional cloud sync is added, this policy will be updated before that version is released, and the change will be described in the app’s release notes.
